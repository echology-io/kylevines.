1. Platform Overview

MEG is a software-first platform for decentralized peer-to-peer energy trading. The platform coordinates five integrated systems to enable secure, intelligent, and auditable energy exchange within regulatory constraints.

Permissioned Blockchain

Auditable, immutable trade settlement on a purpose-built energy chain. Every transaction is verifiable by regulators, participants, and auditors.

AI/ML Intelligence

Demand/supply forecasting, dynamic pricing optimization, anomaly detection, and grid load balancing. AI optimizes the market; it doesn't gatekeep it.

IoT Edge Network

Real-time smart meter data flowing from edge devices through authenticated channels with tamper-evident integrity from source to settlement.

Matching Engine

Automated order matching with transparent price discovery, preference-based routing, and forecast-informed bidding strategies.

Regulatory reality: True P2P energy sales are blocked by utility franchise laws in most US jurisdictions. MEG is architected for behind-the-meter deployment first (campuses, multi-tenant, microgrids), with regulatory expansion pathways built into the design. Every component produces audit artifacts that satisfy regulatory scrutiny.

4. Blockchain Settlement Layer

The settlement layer is the centerpiece. Everything feeds into or settles through it.

4.1 Contract Architecture (4 contracts)

ParticipantRegistry.sol

Role-based access control: Prosumer, Utility, GridOperator, Regulator. W3C DID identity. KYC metadata pointers (IPFS). Plugs into Besu account permissioning if needed.

EnergyToken.sol (ERC-1155)

Each token ID = generation batch (device + time period). Balance = Wh. Mint triggered by oracle after meter validation. Claim/retire for REC compliance. Metadata: source type, location, timestamp.

EnergyMarket.sol

Offer/accept with escrow. Seller locks energy tokens, buyer deposits payment. Atomic settlement releases both sides. Grid fee deduction to GridOperator. Dispute timeout.

Settlement.sol

Periodic settlement (15-min intervals). Validates energy balance (exports ~ imports + losses). Calculates net financial positions. Emits auditable settlement events.

4.2 On-Chain vs Off-Chain

On-Chain (immutable, auditable)

  • Trade records (seller, buyer, kWh, price, period)
  • Net settlement balances per participant
  • Energy attribute certificates (REC/EAC)
  • Meter reading hashes (provenance)
  • Market rules (price floor/ceiling, grid fees)
  • Participant registry and role assignments
  • Dispute resolutions and penalties

Off-Chain (performant, private)

  • Raw meter data (5-15s intervals)
  • Order book / bid-ask matching
  • Weather and forecast data
  • User profiles, KYC documents
  • Real-time grid state
  • ML model weights and training data
  • Encrypted PII (meter-level consumption)

5. AI/ML Intelligence Layer

The intelligence layer runs six distinct ML services. Each operates independently, communicates via Redis Streams, and degrades gracefully - the market functions without AI, but AI makes every decision better.

5.1 Demand/Supply Forecasting

Purpose

Predict energy demand and supply (solar/wind generation) at community and individual prosumer level. Enables forward-looking trade decisions and grid stability planning.

Model Architecture

ComponentMVPProduction
Demand forecastXGBoost (hourly, 24h ahead)Ensemble: XGBoost + LSTM + TFT
Solar generationXGBoost + irradiance dataCNN on satellite imagery + XGBoost
Wind generationProphet + wind speedLSTM with turbine-specific curves
UncertaintyPrediction intervals (quantile regression)DeepAR probabilistic forecasting

Feature Set

  • Temporal: hour, day_of_week, month, is_weekend, is_holiday, minutes_since_midnight
  • Weather: temperature, solar irradiance, cloud cover, humidity, wind speed/direction
  • Lagged: load at t-1h, t-24h, t-168h (same hour last week)
  • Rolling: 24h mean, 24h std, 7-day mean, peak/trough ratio
  • Building: square footage, HVAC type, occupancy schedule (when available)
  • Price: current grid tariff, time-of-use period, demand response signals

5.2 Dynamic Pricing Optimization

Purpose

Optimize energy prices in real-time based on supply/demand forecasts, grid conditions, time-of-use tariffs, and participant preferences. Maximizes market efficiency and grid stability.

Approach

  • Price signal generation: Linear programming (SciPy) minimizes total system cost subject to grid constraints (line capacity, voltage limits, ramp rates)
  • Dynamic tariff: Time-of-use pricing adjusts every 15 minutes based on forecast surplus/deficit
  • Incentive shaping: Price premiums for grid-stabilizing behavior (discharge battery during peak, reduce load during scarcity)
  • Fairness constraints: Price bounds prevent exploitation - floor protects sellers, ceiling protects buyers. Both configurable by GridOperator.

Guardrails

  • Price changes capped at +/- 15% per interval to prevent shock
  • All pricing decisions logged with full input/output trace for regulatory audit
  • Manual override capability for GridOperator in emergency conditions
  • Kill switch: revert to fixed tariff if optimization service is unavailable

5.3 Anomaly Detection

Purpose

Detect meter tampering, data fraud, equipment failure, and unusual consumption patterns in real-time. Critical for settlement integrity and regulatory trust.

Detection Models

ThreatModelSignals
Meter tamperingIsolation ForestSudden reading drops, statistical deviation from neighbors, zero-injection periods
Data injectionLSTM autoencoderReconstruction error on reading sequences, physically impossible values
Equipment failureThreshold + trend analysisFlat readings, communication dropout patterns, voltage/frequency anomalies
Billing fraudClustering (DBSCAN)Consumption patterns inconsistent with building profile, reverse flow without solar
Market manipulationRule-based + statisticalWash trading, coordinated bidding, artificial scarcity

Response Pipeline

Reading ingested
  -> Validate (physical bounds, checksum, timestamp)
  -> Score (anomaly probability 0.0-1.0)
  -> Route:
     score < 0.3  -> Accept, log
     score 0.3-0.7 -> Accept, flag for review, alert GridOperator
     score > 0.7  -> Quarantine, exclude from settlement, alert + investigate
     score > 0.9  -> Quarantine, suspend meter, notify regulator

5.4 Battery Storage Optimization

Purpose

Optimize charge/discharge schedules for battery storage systems (Tesla Powerwall, Enphase, etc.) to maximize prosumer revenue and grid stability.

  • Objective: Minimize electricity cost (or maximize revenue) subject to battery constraints (capacity, C-rate, degradation)
  • Method: Mixed-integer linear programming (OR-Tools) over a rolling 24h horizon
  • Inputs: Price forecast, solar generation forecast, demand forecast, battery state-of-charge, grid tariff schedule
  • Output: 15-minute charge/discharge schedule pushed to battery controller via API
  • Constraint: Battery degradation cost factored into optimization - never trade short-term gains for long-term asset damage

5.5 Grid Load Balancing

Purpose

Coordinate distributed energy resources to maintain grid stability. Prevent congestion, voltage violations, and frequency deviations.

  • Congestion management: If a feeder approaches capacity, pricing signals incentivize load reduction or storage discharge in that zone
  • Voltage regulation: Reactive power dispatch from smart inverters, coordinated by optimization service
  • Frequency support: Fast-response battery dispatch for frequency regulation (ancillary services revenue stream)
  • Demand response: Automated load curtailment signals via OpenADR 2.0b when grid stress exceeds threshold

5.6 Trade Recommendation Engine

Purpose

Personalized trade recommendations for prosumers based on their generation profile, consumption pattern, preferences, and market conditions.

  • Input: Prosumer's forecast, current battery SOC, market prices, preference settings (cheapest, greenest, local-first)
  • Output: Recommended buy/sell actions with confidence scores and projected savings
  • Model: Rule-based engine (MVP) evolving to reinforcement learning (production)
  • Explainability: Every recommendation includes a human-readable rationale. No black-box trading decisions.

6. IoT / Edge Data Pipeline

6.1 Edge Node Architecture

  • Hardware: Raspberry Pi 4 (4GB) + Eastron SDM630 submeter (~$100/node)
  • OS: Raspberry Pi OS Lite (headless), hardened (no SSH password, key-only, fail2ban)
  • Software: Python agent reading Modbus TCP every 15 seconds
  • Local buffer: SQLite ring buffer (24h of readings) for connectivity drops. Backfill on reconnect.
  • Authentication: Mutual TLS (client certificate per device, issued by MEG CA)
  • Integrity: Each reading signed with device key. Hash chain links consecutive readings.
  • Watchdog: systemd watchdog restarts agent on crash. Heartbeat published every 60s. Missing heartbeat triggers platform alert.
  • OTA updates: Signed firmware updates pulled from platform on schedule. Rollback on boot failure.

7. Matching Engine

7.1 Market Modes

Periodic Double Auction (Default)

Every 15 minutes, collect buy bids and sell asks. Find clearing price at supply/demand intersection. All trades execute at clearing price. Transparent and fair.

  • Bids/asks submitted during trading window
  • Window closes at interval boundary
  • Engine sorts: sellers ascending, buyers descending
  • Clearing price = intersection point
  • Matched trades committed to settlement contract

Continuous Bilateral (Optional)

Prosumers set standing preferences: buy local solar first, cheapest first, greenest first. Engine continuously matches as energy is generated.

  • Preference-based routing (not just price)
  • Standing orders with validity periods
  • Partial fills supported
  • Settlement batched to 15-min periods

7.2 Forecast-Informed Bidding

The matching engine integrates with the forecasting service to enable intelligent automated bidding:

  • Auto-bid: Prosumers can enable automated trading. The system generates bids based on their forecast, battery SOC, and price preferences.
  • Price bounds: Auto-bids respect user-configured min sell price and max buy price. No surprises.
  • Forecast confidence: Bid aggressiveness scales with forecast confidence. Low confidence = conservative bids near grid tariff.
  • Override: Manual bids always override auto-bids. Users retain full control.

8. Settlement Economics

How money flows through the platform. This is what investors ask first and regulators scrutinize most.

8.1 Payment Model

MVP: Fiat Settlement (Off-Chain)

Trades are recorded on-chain. Financial settlement is off-chain via ACH/payment rail. The blockchain is the ledger of record; the bank is the payment processor.

  • Prosumer links bank account at registration (Plaid integration)
  • Net settlement calculated per period (15-min intervals, batched daily)
  • Daily net payout/debit via ACH (Stripe Connect or Dwolla)
  • On-chain settlement record serves as the invoice

Lowest regulatory friction

Future: Stablecoin Settlement (On-Chain)

Payment token (USDC or platform stablecoin) settles atomically with energy tokens on-chain. True DvP (delivery versus payment).

  • Prosumer deposits USDC to escrow contract
  • Trade settlement swaps energy token + payment token atomically
  • No counterparty risk - settlement is instant and final
  • Off-ramp to fiat via exchange or payment partner

Requires money transmitter licensing in most states

8.3 Unit Economics (Per Prosumer)

Assumptions (typical US residential solar + storage prosumer):
  Solar generation:    8,000 kWh/year
  Self-consumption:    60% (4,800 kWh)
  Exported to market:  40% (3,200 kWh)
  Average trade price: $0.12/kWh (vs $0.15 grid tariff)
  Grid fee:            $0.03/kWh
  Platform fee:        2%

Revenue per prosumer per year:
  Seller revenue:      3,200 kWh x $0.12          = $384
  Grid fee deducted:   3,200 kWh x $0.03          = ($96)
  Platform fee:        $384 x 2%                   = ($7.68)
  Net seller income:                               = $280.32

Buyer savings per prosumer per year:
  Bought from market:  3,200 kWh x $0.12          = $384
  vs grid purchase:    3,200 kWh x $0.15          = $480
  Savings:                                         = $96/year

MEG platform revenue per prosumer pair: $7.68/year (seller) + $7.68/year (buyer) = ~$15/year
MEG revenue at 10,000 prosumers: ~$150,000/year
MEG revenue at 100,000 prosumers: ~$1.5M/year

SELECT create_hypertable('meter_readings', 'time');

-- Continuous aggregate: 15-min settlement intervals
CREATE MATERIALIZED VIEW meter_readings_15min
WITH (timescaledb.continuous) AS
SELECT
    time_bucket('15 minutes', time) AS bucket,
    meter_id,
    SUM(net_energy_wh) AS net_energy_wh,
    AVG(voltage_v) AS avg_voltage,
    MAX(anomaly_score) AS max_anomaly_score,
    COUNT(*) AS reading_count
FROM meter_readings
GROUP BY bucket, meter_id;

14. Privacy Architecture

Meter-level energy consumption data is classified as personally identifiable information (PII) in California, New York, and several other states. The platform must protect this data while maintaining the transparency required for market operation.

14.1 Data Classification

Data TypeClassificationProtection
Individual meter readingsPII / SensitiveEncrypted at rest (AES-256), access-controlled, consent required
Participant identity (name, email)PIIEncrypted at rest, minimal collection, purpose-limited
KYC documentsSensitive PIIStored with identity provider, not on MEG servers. Hash reference only.
Aggregated load data (community-level)Non-PIIAggregation minimum: 15 participants per bucket (k-anonymity)
Trade records (on-chain)PseudonymousWallet addresses only. No PII on-chain. Mapping is off-chain, access-controlled.
Market prices, volumesPublicAggregated. No individual positions exposed.

14.2 Privacy Controls

  • Consent management: Granular opt-in for data sharing. Default is minimum required for market participation.
  • Data minimization: Only collect what's needed. Raw meter data retained per policy, then aggregated and purged.
  • Right to delete: Participant can request account deletion. Off-chain PII is purged. On-chain records (pseudonymous) persist (blockchain immutability) but mapping is destroyed.
  • Purpose limitation: Meter data used only for settlement, forecasting, and anomaly detection. Not sold, not shared without explicit consent.
  • Differential privacy: When aggregated data is shared externally (utilities, researchers), noise injection ensures no individual can be re-identified.
  • Encryption: All PII encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM) and in transit (TLS 1.3). Column-level encryption on sensitive database fields.

9.2 Audit Trail Architecture

Every action that affects settlement, pricing, or participant status produces an immutable audit record. Regulators get read-only API access to query the full audit history.

Audit Events (logged to PostgreSQL + mirrored on-chain)

  • Meter events: reading received, validated, flagged, quarantined, submitted to chain
  • Trade events: bid placed, ask placed, matched, settled, disputed, resolved
  • Price events: price calculated, bounds applied, manual override, emergency revert
  • Participant events: registered, role changed, suspended, KYC updated
  • System events: model deployed, model rolled back, contract paused, contract upgraded
  • Security events: anomaly detected, device revoked, incident triggered, access denied

Retention

  • On-chain: permanent (settlement records, certificate issuance/retirement, participant registry)
  • Off-chain (PostgreSQL): 7 years minimum (FERC record retention requirements)
  • Raw meter data: 3 years (configurable per jurisdiction)
  • ML artifacts: model versions retained indefinitely, training data 2 years

17.2 Scaling Strategy

Data Layer

  • TimescaleDB: Native time-partitioning (chunk by day). Compression after 7 days (10x reduction). Continuous aggregates eliminate query-time computation.
  • PostgreSQL: Read replicas for API queries. Write primary for ingestion. Connection pooling via PgBouncer.
  • Redis: Cluster mode for pub/sub fan-out. Streams for ordered event processing.

Compute Layer

  • API: Horizontally scalable FastAPI behind load balancer. Stateless. Session state in Redis.
  • Matching engine: Single-writer per market zone (avoids race conditions). Partitioned by grid region.
  • ML services: Horizontally scalable. Model serving is stateless. Batch inference for non-real-time.
  • MQTT: EMQX cluster (replaces Mosquitto at scale). Supports millions of connections.

Blockchain Layer

  • Batching: Individual trades batch into merkle trees. One on-chain tx per settlement period per market zone (not per trade).
  • State channels: High-frequency micro-trades settle via state channels, with periodic on-chain checkpoints.
  • Multi-chain: Regional market zones can run on separate Besu instances with cross-chain bridge for certificate portability.

Edge Layer

  • MQTT topic partitioning: Topics partitioned by region for broker load distribution.
  • Edge compute: Local aggregation on RPi reduces data volume. Only 15-min aggregates published at scale (raw stays local).
  • Fleet management: Balena or AWS IoT Greengrass for OTA updates at scale.

25. What Makes This Defensible

  • Cryptographic data integrity from meter to settlement. Readings are signed at the edge, hash-chained in transit, and anchored on-chain. Tamper-evident at every layer. No other energy trading platform has this end-to-end.
  • AI that optimizes without gatekeeping. The market functions without AI. Forecasting, pricing optimization, and anomaly detection make it better, but they're not single points of failure. Graceful degradation is architecturally enforced.
  • Regulatory-native design. Not "we'll figure out compliance later." Audit trails, data retention, incident response, role-based access, and regulator read-only APIs are built into the foundation. The platform produces the artifacts regulators need.
  • Portable contracts. Solidity on EVM. If a utility demands Besu, we deploy to Besu. If regulation favors public chains, we bridge. If EWC deprecates, we migrate. Zero contract code changes.
  • Behind-the-meter first. Doesn't need utility permission to launch. Doesn't need regulatory approval. Proves the technology, generates real data, and creates the foundation for expansion into utility sandboxes, retail markets, and wholesale aggregation.
  • Explainable AI. Every forecast includes confidence intervals. Every price includes its calculation inputs. Every trade recommendation includes its rationale. Every anomaly flag includes its evidence. Regulators and participants can inspect every decision.
